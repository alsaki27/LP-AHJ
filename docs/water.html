Water crossing decisions

Working methods and approval gates for all mapped route-to-water intersections.

Reviewed November 24, 2026. Stations are feet from the start of each KMZ feature. Final approval pathways depend on the surveyed work, both approaches and access limits.

Standard submission checklist and agency intake

For each crossing, attach the site-specific items listed on its card to this base package.

  • Location map, crossing ID and station, surveyed limits and ROW or work access rights. Show both approaches and rig, pit or bridge access areas.
  • Plan and profile with surveyed structures, drainage and utility clearances, proposed means and methods, and traffic and restoration plans. Add sealed geotechnical or structural design where required.
  • For environmental review, include delineation, effective flood data, and temporary and permanent impact tables. Show drilling fluid handling and contingency where relevant.
  • Ask the applicable Corps district to determine Section 10 and Section 404 and whether NWP 57, pre-construction notification or an individual permit applies. Coordinate state certification and local floodplain review when triggered. An alternate alignment or method needs the same review against its new footprint.
  • For Delaware County, follow the field-dimensioned utility plan checklist and submit the ordinary utility application 4–8 weeks before planned work. Confirm the separate approval process and schedule for bridge work. County utility process
  • For New Albany, the current application checklist includes proposed work drawings, maintenance of traffic plans, insurance, bond and fee. Coordinate the city’s pre-release site visit. City ROW application
