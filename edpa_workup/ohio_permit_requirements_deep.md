# Ohio permit jurisdiction and schedule workpaper — S0 + S1

Prepared 12 November 2026. This is an operational permit-screening supplement to the Segment 0 and Segment 1 analyses. It translates station overlays into owner-facing next steps and distinguishes published rules from schedule allowances that still need confirmation. No agency or permit coordinator was contacted.

## The answer to “which ODOT districts?”

**District 6 is the only ODOT district with candidate ODOT state-highway route occupancy/crossing contacts along the supplied Ohio alignment.** The route-centerline screen finds potential state-road proximity windows on D6-owned route inventory segments listed below. These are road-centerline candidates within the source GIS search band; they do not establish the proposed fiber is in ODOT ROW or that a D6 utility permit is legally required at those stations.

**District 5 is the geographic ODOT district for Licking County, but the current screen does not identify a State-classified ODOT road centerline near the proposed route in the Licking County stretch.** Licking County lies in ODOT D5; Delaware, Franklin, and Union counties lie in D6. That county/district association is not the same as a district permit obligation. For Licking portions, the route road screen instead surfaced county, township, municipal and private road candidates. D5 becomes a permit contact only if a verified State-owned/maintained route or ODOT facility/ROW is found there, or ODOT confirms a special crossing/structure jurisdiction.

ODOT's current permit page states permits are needed for work in ODOT ROW, including utility installation and soil borings; the issued permit is limited to the state ROW limits stated on the permit. The same page routes work within corporation limits to the city/village and says county/township ROW permits must be obtained from those authorities. Therefore the controlling permit is **not** “ODOT district permit for every mile within that district.” It is the road/ROW owner at the specific station. For candidate State ROW, begin with District 6 Right-of-Way/Utility permit staff and obtain written direction where the line is in an incorporated city, limited-access ROW, bridge/structure or existing ODOT project. ODOT page currently lists Utilities Manager Wendi Snyder at 614-728-8062; District 6 office is 740-833-8000. These are public contacts, not agency feedback on this project.

## ODOT utility-permit pathway and submittal package

### Trigger and agency boundary

1. If any excavation, bore pit, conduit, cable, soil boring, access or other work occupies acquired ODOT ROW, initiate a Right-of-Way & Utility permit in the current ePermitting system. Create a MyODOT user/customer account first. ODOT's public page describes utility work as permit work and lists soil borings separately.
2. If the work is in a city/village corporation limit, request that municipality's written permit-owner determination. ODOT's current page says city/village approval controls there. Also ask the city whether ODOT D6/bridge concurrence remains necessary at the specific state route or structure.
3. If only crossing a state highway, do not assume a standard “outside the pavement” exception: confirm whether pits/access/workspaces occupy ODOT ROW and whether highway/access control or bridge ownership adds approval. Permit is required for work in ROW; route crossing plans must show the entire work limits.
4. County/township roadway segments route to the county or township authority, not D6 just because the road lies in an ODOT district county. Private roads, private easements and City of Columbus reservoir property require separate owner rights.

### ODOT file to prepare (conservative vendor-ready package; confirm attachment checklist in portal)

ODOT's public permit landing page gives the permit trigger and ePermitting account path but does **not** publish a fiber-specific plan-sheet checklist, fee schedule, or utility review SLA. The ePermitting user guide requires application fields and uploaded attachments (plans/profile) plus mapping the asset location and line segments. The ODOT Location & Design Manual Volume 1 Section 802 says formal application/necessary agreements must be complete before work. ODOT's Real Estate Manual Section 8100 governs utility accommodation. Build one complete package rather than submit a route sketch:

- Signed applicant/utility-owner authorization and project contact; requested work type, permanent/temporary status, county, route feature, exact station/logpoint and construction window.
- Vicinity map and station-controlled plan sheets identifying the road, ODOT route and control section, corporate limits, ODOT ROW lines from the current acquisition plan, road/edge/shoulder, bridge/culvert/interchange/access-control limits, property/parcel lines, proposed fiber route, crossings, bore pits, handholes, vaults, splice points, access and restoration limits.
- Proposed longitudinal and crossing profiles: horizontal and vertical alignment, proposed cover/depth, casing/conduit, separation/clearances, bore entry/exit geometry, conflicts, and all proposed facilities/appurtenances. Show full work envelope not just cable centerline.
- Existing utility depiction: show available utility-owner records, OUPS/811 design tickets where available, visible surface features, known public/private utilities, drainage, and SUE confidence by segment. For this corridor bid, use QL-B designation for congested/highway corridors as a planning baseline and QL-A test holes at conflict points, bridge ends, shallow crossings and proposed pit locations. This is a prudent design recommendation, not a claim that ODOT requires QL-B/A on every private utility permit. The current ODOT L&D Volume 4 SUE spec says QL-D is required on ODOT design projects when known utilities are to be shown, while QL A-C may be required case-by-case; that design-project rule is not automatically the private permit standard.
- Traffic-control/MOT sheets, work sequencing, lane/shoulder closures, pedestrian access, emergency access, restoration standard/details, erosion/stormwater controls and maintenance contact. For interstate/interchange or bridge effects, provide specialty traffic/structural review basis and alternatives.
- Technical attachments: geotechnical assumptions, HDD calculations/profile, frac-out prevention/response if relevant, environmental permits/conditions, railroad concurrence where near/under rail, bridge or culvert owner approvals, and evidence of property/right-to-enter authority.
- Coordinate known utilities before final design. Ohio Revised Code 3781.27 requires developers/designers to request utility information, show approximate underground facility locations, identify utility contacts and adjustments/time, and design to avoid disturbance; for new underground utilities, statute says attempt to provide at least 12-inch clearance. 811 notification before excavation is separate from design-level locating and does not replace SUE.

ODOT-specific constraints to flag early: ODOT policy generally disallows longitudinal overhead lines in limited-access ROW; limited-access occupancy can be prohibited or require exceptions and alternate-route justification; ODOT discourages bridge attachment, and says bridge installation needs District Production Office, District Utility Coordinator and bridge-office approval, only if no reasonable alternative. Keep ODOT bridge installs out of the base bid until accepted. Work and final restoration must conform to current ODOT standards/permit notes.

### Timeline, fee and delay statements that can actually be supported

- **ODOT utility permit review:** no published standard or expedited utility-permit decision duration found on ODOT's current permit page/manual entry. Do not repeat the SHAMM's 30-calendar-day target: that text applies to access-permit requests after a correct and complete application, not a fiber utility accommodation. No current official general utility permit fee was identified on the public page; do not assert “free.” Confirm fee, bonds, insurance, permit route and actual review target through current ePermitting/D6 before bid close. Budget time for additional reviews/conditions where limited access, bridges, ramps, multiple counties/cities, environmental crossing or missing ROW plans apply.
- **Permitting schedule allowance (vendor estimate, not agency SLA):** after survey/SUE and owner rights are resolved, carry 8–12 weeks for a complete routine local/ODOT application round and response, plus 4–8 weeks for each material resubmittal/route change. Carry 3–6+ months where freeway/limited-access exception, bridge, City water property, railroad, environmental authorization, or multiple authority concurrence is needed. Do not count this as regulator commitment; convert to confirmed agency clocks after pre-application meetings/complete submittals.
- **City of Columbus:** utility excavation uses 903 ROW Excavation Permit and a plan-routing review. Its checklist has strict plan/profile, pit, offset, crossing orientation, existing-utility clearance, stationing and 811 note requirements (see utility depiction section below); no 903 plan-review SLA was found. City Code Ch. 906 is for street/sidewalk occupancy rather than the permanent utility excavation permit: applications 5 working days ahead (10 for full lane closure), decision within 5 working days with written extension up to another 5. Do not apply the 906 clock to permanent utility permit. Columbus rules prohibit openings in new/re-paved streets for 3 years except emergency repairs and openings in Special Improved Streets for 5 years; joint use of facilities is required on Special Improved Streets if adequate capacity exists. Route through these streets may require bore reroute, shared duct negotiation or hold until moratorium expires.
- **Delaware County:** for its public utility permit, the controlling Public Utility Permit Process says submit **at least 4 weeks but no more than 8 weeks before work** (this is a required filing window, not a decision SLA). Current county page lists utility fee “NO fee,” but inspection may be charged when required. Plan must dimension existing pavement/ROW and proposed work from pavement edge; identify utility length/size, identifiable station objects, installation method; for work over 200 ft dimension both ends. Hard-surface road crossings must be bored unless separately authorized for pavement cutting. No work within 5 ft of pavement edge; minimum road-crossing cover is 48 in. For all projects the applicant/contractor employs a full-time inspector (100%); traffic control and restoration are permittee responsibilities; contact county 2 working days before start. Permit valid one year. City streets go to cities, state/interstate roads to ODOT, county/township roads outside city to county; Concord, Genoa and Liberty townships issue selected permits themselves (Concord/Liberty include ROW permits). This 4–8 week lead is a major schedule constraint and needs to be converted to a route sequencing hold point; check the current application for insurance and inspection fee details.
- **Union County:** published ROW requirements say permits generally approved within 2 working days; application generally submitted not less than 3 days (excluding weekends/holidays) before start. The application and plans are required before work; separate applications are required for non-contiguous work unless a blanket permit is issued at the Engineer’s discretion. Plans for underground work are required and explicitly show existing road centerline, ROW, pavement edge, bridge/culvert, utility lines, drainage structures, fences, swales, catch basins, manholes, other features; proposed work, size/depth/details, and profiles/elevations as required. Published minimum cover: 3 ft below pavement, 2.5 ft below sod ditches, and 2 ft below paved ditches/other surfaces. Open-cut is generally only permitted on surface-treated roads and underground lines larger than 16 in on HMA; <=16-in lines crossing HMA must be installed by another method approved by Engineer, making bore/HDD the expected fiber crossing approach. Maintain traffic; notify Sheriff and affected fire departments 2 days before road closure; notify Engineer 2 days before start, restoration inspection, stoppage and resumption. Permit expires after 120 days unless a different term/extension. The two-day approval target assumes compliant complete material; no fee schedule found in reviewed requirements.
- **Licking County:** the official utility application/instructions state **pavement cuts are not permitted**; a cable must be buried at least 36 in below pavement, ditch/channel and around cross-culverts/bridges; attach two copies of proposed plans referencing pavement, ROW lines, property lines, cross-section where the service crosses under pavement, and drainage structures. The permit states maintain traffic per OMUTCD and provide barricades/warning devices/flaggers as needed, restore topsoil/sod, and call the Highway Department **24 hours before** start. A sample/currently issued utility permit makes work complete by Dec. 1 and bars County ROW work **Dec. 1–Apr. 15** unless the County Engineer approves an exception. Treat that seasonal bar as a schedule hard stop on county-road segments until the current general permit form and route-specific conditions confirm it. Official permit page offers the current Right-of-Way Work Permit and names the permit contact, but the reviewed pages do not state a utility review SLA or fee. Request those plus whether two-copy plan rule, 36-in cover and moratorium apply to all road surfaces or by permit. D5 only if final alignment enters ODOT ROW there.
- **Franklin County:** Engineer has a public road-ROW utility authorization/application and a county permit form. The surfaced current public material does not provide a reliable utility plan-review SLA or consolidated fee schedule. An archived county permit form requires restoration, MOT, call the day before start, and permit expires 30 days after issuance unless work starts; treat that as a permit-validity condition only if confirmed current. A County-wide EDP notice of three-week development review is not the Engineer’s utility permit clock and is excluded.
- **Townships:** ORC 5571.16 lets township trustees regulate excavation/culvert work in township highways and set plans, methods, safeguards, indemnity and bonds/deposits by resolution. Delaware’s permit page identifies Concord and Liberty (also Genoa) as township ROW permit issuers; everywhere else, confirm whether County Engineer administers or township issues its own permit. Township boundary alone does not establish township-road ROW.

## “Do they want all utilities shown?” — bid rule that prevents resubmittals

**For this route, design and submit with all known existing utilities shown on every applicable authority's plan set.** Do not interpret “not expressly required on the web form” as permission to omit. Requirements differ:

- Union County explicitly requires construction plans for underground work and its plan content expressly includes existing utility lines (along with centerline, ROW, pavement edge, bridges/culverts, drainage, etc.). It also requires plan/profile/elevations as applicable.
- ODOT public web page does not enumerate a utility-plan drawing checklist. The portal requests plan/profile attachments; submit existing and proposed utilities because conflicts and ROW encroachment cannot otherwise be reviewed. This is recommended completeness, not a separately confirmed posted ODOT rule.
- Delaware County utility application gives a plan checklist around pavement, ROW, dimensions, utility type/size, location and method; it does not expressly list “show all existing utilities” in the text surfaced. The route-owner check should nevertheless request a utility depiction standard, and plans must show known drainage conflicts; include all known existing utility facilities for completeness. The application imposes 4–8 week filing window, 100% contractor-employed inspection, 5-ft pavement offset, 48-in road crossing cover and bore crossing on hard-surface roads.
- Franklin County's form requests authorization to install a utility “along” or “across” a named road and references accompanying plans; exact drawing requirements not clear from the surfaced form. Treat existing utilities, drainage district tiles, pipe crossings and profiles as mandatory design content, then obtain county checklist.
- Columbus 903 excavation-plan checklist explicitly wants scaled plan/profile (minimum 1 in = 40 ft, with horizontal and vertical scales), proposed conduit offsets from ROW/road centerline, pits dimensioned, station breaks where no work, bore/push/open-cut method, and a note requiring at least 3-ft horizontal and 1-ft vertical clearance from existing utilities. It requires perpendicular utility crossings and alignment consistently parallel to ROW/road centerline to minimize City utility crossings; deviations need explanation. It does not say all existing utilities must be plotted in the checklist text; nonetheless existing facilities and utilities have to be surveyed/located to demonstrate required clearances and eliminate conflicts. City plan-routing manual reserves department-specific requirements. City rules 906 occupancy are distinct from utility excavation 903.
- Licking County utility application expressly requires two copies of plans that reference pavement, ROW, property lines, cross-sections when crossing under pavement and drainage structures. It does not explicitly say each existing private utility must be plotted; the complete plan should still show all utilities identified by owner records/SUE so conflicts are reviewable. It forbids pavement cuts and contains the 36-in cover and seasonal restriction described above.

Recommended universal utility depiction: existing utility owner/type/material/size where known, horizontal line, depth/elevation and quality level, source/date/confidence, visible facilities, OUPS markings, proposed fiber/casing, bore profile and cover, pit footprint, offsets to ROW/edge of pavement/drainage/structures, crossings/separation, conflicts, mitigation and utility-owner concurrence. Show a legend with dashed/solid or confidence symbology; do not present record-only locations as surveyed. Preserve source/timestamp and embed station values.
