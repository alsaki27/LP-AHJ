## Executive read

The images do **not** reveal a clearly new, previously unregistered crossing of a major river or railroad. They reinforce the existing Salt Creek crossing, the Spur4 NWI drainage lead, and the six separately mapped CJR rail intersections. They also show three material follow-ups:

1. **Salt Creek is a broad, wooded floodplain and an existing road-bridge interface.** The route target falls at an apparent road bridge; water/wooded corridor continues beyond the pavement and the approaches are near residential and large institutional/recreation/commercial parcels. No bridge utility attachment is identifiable in the NAIP crop. Treat a bridge attachment as an unapproved option requiring owner/structural review; retain a below-channel/approach HDD alternative until the owner supplies bridge plans and rights.
2. **The NWI-only Spur4 drainage is not visibly open water, but there is a narrow tree/vegetation corridor at the mapped point.** It lies among warehouses and pavement and appears consistent with a ditch/swale or buried/culverted conveyance. The available view cannot tell if it has flow, a culvert, wetland hydrology, or jurisdiction. This is a real survey and staging gate, not grounds to delete the NWI lead.
3. **Two rail target crops do not visually show a track centered on the plotted pin.** At T10 and T13 the pin appears at an industrial street junction while the strongest linear rail corridor is offset elsewhere in the crop. Because the GIS register calls both exact route/rail intersections and maps them near Touhy crossings, reconcile route KMZ stationing, rail geometry, image georeferencing, and crossing IDs before design or permit counts. No corrected location is asserted here.

## Rail corridor review

The visible industrial landscape around T05, T06, and T09–T14 has multiple distinct E–W rail corridors, with warehouses, loading docks, fenced yards and short local streets close to the track frontage. T09, T11 and T12 show successive rail bands in the same broader industrial area; the separate lines appear to align with separately registered rail stations rather than one overlooked compound track crossing. The crops are not sharp enough to count every main track, switch, signal, siding or out-of-service track. The six FRA/NARN interactions stay six separate screening nodes until track charts, current railroad data, and owner review prove otherwise.

- **T09 / T11 / T12:** imagery shows multiple parallel rail corridors and at-grade-looking street interfaces; one rail band lies near each pin, with a separate parallel band farther south. Maintain separate crossing cases and verify exact stationing.
- **T14:** rail corridor visibly crosses a local industrial street beside warehouse/parking frontage; the apparent at-grade interface has direct truck/drive access implications.
- **T10 / T13:** target-to-track visual alignment is inconclusive. These are **location QA flags**, not new crossings or evidence the GIS candidates are wrong. The image register should be corrected or recaptured only after a GIS overlay verifies the pin.
- **Near-parallel lead:** the prior screen found CJR FRAARCID 513744 69.9 ft from Spur3, plus farther proximity candidates. The NAIP views support a rail-dense industrial corridor but cannot establish railroad parcel width or fee ownership. Keep the 69.9-ft lead on the ROW/property screening list and examine staging/pits near it.

## Water, drainage, and environmental screen

- **Salt Creek (T02/T07):** visible open channel/river and wooded floodplain at a paved bridge crossing. The width of the wooded corridor and nearby recreation/institutional, residential and developed uses make access, staging and restoration nontrivial. No additional named waterway crossing is clear in these two crops.
- **Spur4 NWI lead (T08):** target falls at a narrow vegetated strip within a highly built industrial area, with road pavement at/near the corridor. The strip may be a drainage swale, culvert approach or a different green parcel boundary; imagery does not confirm open water. Ask for ditch/culvert survey and drainage-owner records before freezing the alignment.
- **Other midpoint crops (T01, T03–T06):** no obvious unregistered open-water crossing is visible at the pin. T03 is close to a broad wooded/open-space area, so check the route/road ROW against park/forest-preserve parcel lines and tree/restoration requirements even though the source wetland layer did not report an intersection.

## Constructability patterns along the road segments

- **T01 and T04:** residential streets meet commercial/warehouse uses; street trees, driveways and many intersections make curb/parkway availability and continuous trench access uncertain.
- **T03:** road edge is beside a large forested/open-space block and residences; confirm public-land boundary, tree restrictions, and contractor access.
- **T05–T06 and T09–T14:** industrial warehouses, truck courts, loading docks, and rail corridors dominate. Expect local access, construction staging and daily work-hour constraints. T05 shows a visibly graded/cleared parcel, so obtain current development/traffic plans before assuming it remains a usable staging area.
- No visible pole/casing detail is reliable enough at NAIP scale to establish spare duct, existing utility attachment, overhead clearance, or subsurface utility clearance.
