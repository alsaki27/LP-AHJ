# Segment 1 — NAIP visual review

**Route:** Marysville–New Albany (Segment 1), source alignment S1-S028 through S1-S046  
**Review date:** 23 November 2026  
**Review scope:** 41 indexed image crops covering the route and flagged water, rail, ODOT structure and highway-interface targets. This is a visual screening supplement to `segment1_imagery_gap_audit.md`; it does not amend the route, parcel or permit registers.

## Director’s read

The imagery corroborates the two mapped CSX crossing locations, shows the scale and approach constraints at the US 33/US 36 interchange, and confirms that the S1-S033 Home Road crossing is a long road bridge/causeway across the full width of O’Shaughnessy Reservoir. That reservoir crossing is the most consequential constructability question in this segment: the engineering concept needs to distinguish bridge attachment from any alternative route around the reservoir and establish bridge/road ownership, attachment rules and limits before cost and schedule can be treated as firm.

At S1-S037, the crop reveals a large active quarry/aggregate operation, heavy access and several pond/settling features near the Watkins Road structure target. This is a material access, parcel, safety, staging and drainage coordination issue. The image does **not** establish that the proposed utility route crosses a quarry pond or regulated water.

The 14 additional ODOT bridge/ditch/stream candidates identified in the first-pass register review are generally supported as **wooded drainage, lowland, pond or road-crossing settings** in the NAIP crops. Many channels are hidden beneath tree canopy. Treat each as a separate screening candidate until structure plans, route-level survey and field review establish whether the proposed facility actually intersects a channel, wetland or bridge envelope. This is a real register-completeness issue for due diligence, but not 14 confirmed water permits.

No additional railroad alignment beyond the two already identified CSX locations was visually evident in the reviewed crops. Four route features close to the mapped rail arcs remain proximity/ROW flags rather than additional confirmed crossings. At the Marysville crossing, imagery suggests a wider multi-line rail environment than a single simple track; current track ownership/count and property limits require railroad records.

## Findings by issue

### Home Road at O’Shaughnessy Reservoir — critical

**Target:** S1-S033-Water / ODOT SFN 2134462; 40.196861, -83.139528; approximately route mile 9.00.  
**Image:** `naip_snapshots/segment1_S1-S033-Water_2134462_40.19686_-83.13953.jpg` (also `naip_contact_02.jpg`).

The image plainly shows Home Road crossing open water on a long bridge/causeway, with reservoir water on both sides. The western approach is wooded; the eastern tie-in is near SR 257 and a roundabout. Docks/marina structures are visible south of the crossing. This confirms that the feature should be engineered and priced as a reservoir/bridge-interface problem, not treated as an ordinary narrow stream crossing.

**Vendor implication:** establish the exact proposed attachment location and whether it is on a bridge superstructure, bridge approach, roadway embankment, or separate public/private right-of-way. Obtain the bridge/road owner’s plans and written attachment criteria; check bridge structural limits, access and work windows, approach transitions, shoreline/environmental constraints, navigation/recreation use, dam or water-control infrastructure, and an alternate route concept. The imagery does not identify the bridge owner, dam operator, title, permit authority, bridge condition, structure limits or allowable attachment.

### Quarry / Watkins Road — major constructability flag

**Target:** S1-S037-Water / S1-S038-Bore; SFN 8031045; 40.213699, -83.202263; approximately route mile 13.62.  
**Image:** `naip_snapshots/segment1_S1-S037-Water_8031045_40.21370_-83.20226.jpg` (also `naip_contact_03.jpg`).

The crop shows an active aggregate/quarry footprint next to the road target: excavation, processing/stockpiles, industrial access and several pond/settling-water features. This creates a likely property/access, operational safety, heavy-haul traffic, work-hour, staging and drainage interface. The crop does not resolve ownership, the exact route-to-quarry boundary, whether any visible impoundment is a process pond or regulated water, or whether the proposed alignment crosses one.

**Vendor implication:** overlay surveyed alignment and parcel boundaries; identify operator and landowner from authoritative records; protect active haul/access routes; request site drainage/pond plans and utility records; assess whether a public-road crossing or private parcel/easement is implicated. Keep water-permit status unassigned pending field and agency review.

## Imagery record and limitations

All crops were sourced from the USGS / USDA FSA NAIP imagery service through The National Map. The index reports service metadata date `2025-01-09`; this is explicitly **not** a verified per-pixel acquisition date. Snapshots were retrieved 23 November 2026. Each image is a 900 × 650 m crop centered on a coordinate from the screening register, not a surveyed crossing point. The index CSV contains source URL, coordinate, map-view URL, retrieval timestamp and caution for each image. Contact sheets `naip_contact_01.jpg` through `naip_contact_06.jpg` provide route-wide review context; the individual image filename for each row is listed in `naip_visual_findings.csv`.

This review records visual land cover and visible infrastructure only. Imagery cannot establish parcel title, railroad/bridge/road owner, public ROW, jurisdiction, regulated-water boundaries, wetlands, permit applicability, utility locations, easements, structure condition, track count or construction access rights. No Google imagery was captured or reproduced in this review. The source imagery is NAIP and its acquisition date remains unresolved.
