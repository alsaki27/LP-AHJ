# Chicago spur permitting: route-station gates, package contents, schedule and cost constraints

Prepared 12 November 2026 from the supplied route KMZ and official Illinois / local authority material. This is the operational permit screen the earlier general AHJ list lacked. It ties agency triggers to route stations and separates **confirmed map evidence** from **permit determination**. No authority has been contacted.

## Decision in one minute

- **IDOT District 1 is the state district for any work in IDOT State ROW in Cook or DuPage County.** The current District 1 Utility Permit Guide expressly covers Cook and DuPage. Three state-road centerline intersections are exact route geometry candidates and should be treated as likely D1 crossing permits until ROW / road plans prove otherwise: Spur1-S03 at Higgins Road station 7,224.2–7,428.0 ft; Spur3-S01 at Busse Road 11,707.3–11,907.3 ft; Spur4-S01 at Busse Road 9,074.5–9,274.5 ft. These are permit **planning gates**, not an agency-issued determination. Arlington Heights Road has additional IDOT-coded proximity bands on Spur1-S03 (roughly stations 7,255–9,411 ft) with centerline offsets generally 67–80 ft; verify State ROW limits before treating as actual occupancy.
- **Elk Grove Village is the dominant local permitting authority.** The boundary screen places approximately 48,181 ft of the 49,155-ft Chicago route in Elk Grove Village; Spur1-S01 station 730.5–1,704.2 ft (974 ft) is in Itasca. The feature station 0–730.5 ft is in DuPage County but outside the Itasca polygon in this overlay. Exact municipal ROW/work footprint still controls, not the corporate boundary.
- **Cook County DoTH permit is triggered only by work in an actual Cook County highway ROW.** Current County instructions say apply 2–3 months before needed, include a $100 nonrefundable application fee, and list a $50,000 bond/insurance package for multiple private-utility permits. Exact County route/section must be established on its jurisdiction map. Strong route-station leads include Devon Avenue on Spur1-S01 at ~1,603–1,803 ft and Landmeier Road on Spur1-S03 at ~3,129–3,343 ft (both road centerlines touch route geometry in the public GIS screen). Arlington Heights Road is an additional County or IDOT-coded lead, depending on the exact station segment.
- **Do not price the Elk Grove Village ROW route as routine.** Current Village code requires a $1,500 utility ROW application fee unless a franchise/license agreement changes it, engineered drawings, traffic-control and emergency-contingency plans, insurance, and a security fund. Its code-referenced Electric Utility Design Standards Manual sets $500/linear foot for a duct package running parallel under roadway; a perpendicular road crossing by directional bore is expressly exempt from that fee. The source manual is titled for electric utility design; the code makes utility permits subject to its fee section. **Obtain written Village ruling on whether/how this applies to this telecom duct design, which candidate lengths are treated as under-road parallel, and whether an existing franchise/license changes the fees before locking price.** This is a potential bid-killer if the conceptual path has long longitudinal under-pavement occupation.
- **Rail/water remain separate gates.** UP Real Estate/CJR operating permission is needed at rail intersections; Salt Creek and the Spur4 mapped intermittent streambed require channel/floodway field checks and conditional water approvals. IDOT/city/county roadway permit does not grant railroad license, water authorization, or property rights.

## Authority-by-authority permit playbook

### 1. IDOT District 1 — Higgins Road and Busse Road crossings; possible Arlington Heights Road ROW

**Trigger.** District 1 written utility permission is required for utility construction/maintenance/modification/removal within IDOT State highway ROW, including related staging, temporary access, work zone or traffic control—even if construction stays outside pavement. State ROW is not the same as a State route name or road-centerline proximity. A crossing below a State highway still uses State ROW and should be treated as permit-required absent a written IDOT determination.

**Application pack.** Permittee must be the utility owner or municipality owning/operating the facilities, not the consultant or construction contractor. Use current OPER 1113. Identify State route, municipality, county, nearest-cross-street “from–to” limits, ROW station limits, installation method, size/type/material, anticipated traffic impacts, public improvement/non-public improvement, and separate applications if a permit crosses a county boundary. Scalable legible engineered plans need clear limits, proposed utility and bore profile, trenchless launch/receive pits, surface disturbances, and utilities/facilities to extent reasonably available; dimension utility to the legal ROW line and one other fixed roadway reference. Include traffic-control/MOT plan and make staging limits match. Include special conditions at intake: bridge attachment, standards variance, access-controlled facility, detour or non-standard/overnight hours, environmental sensitivity, winter moratorium exception. Combine into one optimized PDF; keep revisions on same chain and use the assigned reference number.

**Utility location disclosure.** The D1 Guide says plans must show existing utilities/facilities to extent reasonably available; the permittee is responsible to ascertain and avoid underground/aboveground facilities, and IDOT/JULIE must be contacted. D1 provides possible permit records but is not the authoritative location source. For this fiber bid, use quality-level SUE appropriate to design risk and owner standards: incorporate owner atlases, JULIE design tickets/field survey, record drawings, and targeted test holes at crossing/conflict points; show source/date/confidence and unresolved utility conflicts. Do not label an 811 locate as design-grade SUE.

**Timeline / delay.** The published guide gives a process, but no standard number of review days; it explicitly says workflow timing depends on nature, complexity and coordination. Budget multiple review cycles rather than quoting a guaranteed cycle. The manual lists frequent delay triggers: multi-county or Area Coordinator work filed as one permit, inconsistent application/plans, unclear from-to/limits, plans not dimensioned from ROW line and another fixed reference, no traffic plan, traffic plan not aligned with actual staging, oversized/slow files, and late winter/bridge/access-control/variance identification. District 1 may split long continuous work by length/location/coordination; no fixed maximum length is established.

**Seasonal constraint.** The D1 guide describes a winter moratorium **November 1–April 15** for pavement open cuts, HDD observation holes, and lane closures. Exceptions are case-by-case; no snow/ice or forecast snow unless true emergency, overnight closures need explicit advance authorization, daily restore/backfill, steel plates only if specifically authorized, temporary restoration must be permanently restored next construction season. Existing permit approval before winter does not automatically authorize work during the moratorium. Avoid scheduling the exact Busse/Higgins crossing windows during that period unless an exception is in hand.

### 2. Elk Grove Village — dominant local utility ROW authority

The source routes mostly fall within the Village boundary; boundary placement is not conclusive proof that Village owns every underlying highway. Elk Grove’s current code defines fiber/cable as facilities and requires a permit before a new facility, relocation, change that disrupts the ROW, or materially increased occupied space. Its code allows a franchise, license, or similar agreement to govern if one exists; identify whether the proposing carrier/Lightpath has an agreement and if it extends to these facilities/this utility owner.

**Application, utilities shown, insurance/security.** Code §8-7-9-4 requires utility application, engineer-certified drawings/specifications, written traffic-control plan consistent with Illinois MUTCD, emergency-contingency plan (including construction/hazardous-material emergencies and Village notification), insurance, security fund, variance requests, and other information Village reasonably requires. New communications system applications also need any required ICC or other regulatory authorization. Annual registration/contact/insurance info applies to ROW-occupying utilities. Required insurance absent franchise: $5m per person bodily injury, $5m property damage per accident, $5m other liability, $1m auto, statutory workers’ comp, $1m employer liability, plus $5m umbrella; utility can cover contractor or contractor must comply. Security amount is reasonably estimated restoration cost as set by Community Development Director; phased work may qualify for one security equal to the greatest phase, in the Director’s discretion.

**Engineering limits that affect route choice:** underground parallel comms is to be as near the ROW line as practicable and no more than 5 ft from ROW line; new facility under paved highway only if other locations impracticable or inconsistent with sound engineering (e.g. use existing conduit); roadway crossing as near 90° as practicable, design for maintenance-free life/foreseeable capacity, not in a cattle pass/culvert/drainage facility. Village can require a facility use a particular non-highway ROW instead; attachments to bridges/roadway structures are only available after demonstrating other routing methods impracticable. Direct the fiber along the parkway/easement when viable and bore perpendicular under pavement to cross streets. Do not base a longitudinal street-centerline conduit path on presumed permit acceptance.

**Fee and change risk.** Code fee is $1,500 application unless exempted by franchise/license/similar agreement; code says all permits are subject to utility fees in the Electric Utility Design Standards Manual. That current manual sets a $500/LF fee for a duct package installed parallel under a roadway and $1,000/LF if Village must relocate its water main (per the manual). Fee does not apply to duct package installed by directional boring for perpendicular crossing only. Since manual nomenclature says “Electric Utility” while code imports its fee section for all permits, get a written determination from Community Development specific to fiber duct package, parallel vs cross configuration, installed length basis, and existing franchise/license. Do not hard-code the fee against all 48,181 route feet; only roadway-parallel duct segments may be implicated, and actual in-ROW route length has not been established.

**Published review clock.** Village marketing/application material says an engineering plan-review response is 10 working days or less in almost all cases, but it explicitly is not a guarantee of permit issuance; this is a broad engineering-plan response estimate, not a guaranteed ROW utility permit SLA. Use as the best published planning benchmark for complete uncomplicated local permit packages, not for unusual utility franchise, variances, fee determinations, ROW title, bridge, rail, water, or multi-jurisdiction coordination. Confirm current ROW-specific SLA.

### 3. Itasca — Spur1-S01 station 730.5–1,704.2 ft (974 ft) in municipal boundary

The route boundary overlay puts station 0–1,704 ft in DuPage County; Itasca is station 730.5–1,704.2 ft (974 ft), then Elk Grove Village. Station 0–730.5 ft is outside the Itasca corporate polygon. Itasca Utility Permit Project Requirements expressly ask for (a) existing utilities crossing/parallel/close to proposed route; (b) length and work type by segment—open cut, directional bore or overhead; (c) offset from curb/sidewalk/building/fixed feature; (d) nearby floodplain/wetlands; (e) tree/fire-hydrant protection; (f) ROW and utility easements; (g) traffic-control notes; and a new permit application on resubmittals. Its standard notes require 5-ft horizontal separation from Village utilities and full-depth pavement/backfill and landscape restoration within 14 days of disturbance. No utility-specific review clock or fee was located in its handout; ask on intake. Itasca general permit FAQs say most permits take 10–14 days depending on scope, but that is not a utility-specific commitment. Route feature road mapping also identifies road district candidates here: confirm if any township is actual owner/controller before assuming village/county only.

### 4. Cook County DoTH — only where the route enters a County highway ROW

County DoTH application is required for any work in its highway ROW; its official list explicitly includes fiber optic cable, cable/conduit, road closures, detours, soil borings and similar utility work. Applicant should use County jurisdiction map and identify exact County road and section. Electronic PDF submittals. **Official scheduling advice: apply 2–3 months in advance.** The current web page lists a $100 nonrefundable application fee; separate permit fees set by current County Code ordinance. County utility packet includes a $50,000 bond/insurance path for multiple private-utility permits; verify number/form/insurance terms for this owner and package. County permit applies only to approved work on County ROW; it does not replace other permits. Permit requires two days’ advance notice before field work. Current page names specific utility agreement users (ComEd, AT&T North Shore Gas, Nicor); do not assume Lightpath falls under those agreements. Strong exact route-station GIS leads are Devon at Spur1-S01 ~1,603–1,803 ft and Landmeier at Spur1-S03 ~3,129–3,343 ft; check County road jurisdiction map and ROW deeds/road plans. For Arlington Heights Road, road class changes along the route and the route may be outside the County/State ROW at some candidate spans; station against ROW map before assignment.

### 5. DuPage County DOT / road district — boundary or code-3 candidates only

About 1,704 ft of Spur1-S01 is in DuPage County; the Itasca place polygon covers feature stations 730.5–1,704.2 ft (973.69 ft), then Elk Grove Village starts. Feature station 0–730.5 ft is outside Itasca boundary; check this reach for unincorporated county road or road-district authority. Local roads and ROW likely govern most work. County presence does not itself mean County permit: use DuPage DOT current jurisdiction map and road section/ROW plans; code-3 Arlington Heights Road candidate has centerline offsets of roughly 38–67 ft in portions of this feature. Verify ROW station-by-station. No current official utility permit standard fee/review clock was resolved in this desktop research; do not reuse historical DuPage fee data as a current quote. Contact/records request remains a bid closeout item. Township/road-district code-9 candidates exist but only trigger a separate permit if their road ownership/control is confirmed.

## Workable schedule model (do not add all clocks as if parallel certainty)

**Current due diligence to RFP bid:** one week for road/ROW stationing and evidence collection; 2–3 weeks is likely for a permit-ready preliminary crossing/utility exhibit, subject to client-provided owner atlases and route endpoints. **Post-award permitting:** use published minimum planning anchors only:

- Cook County: submit 2–3 months ahead of required construction (published lead-time advice).
- Elk Grove Village: ~10 working days for initial engineering plan review response in most cases, but no guaranteed utility ROW issuance time; allocate 2–4 weeks for local application, comment response, and fee/franchise resolution as a planning allowance, not a published promise.
- Itasca: 10–14 calendar/business day basis is not established; generic FAQ says most permits 10–14 days, utility-specific clock absent; conservatively plan 3–4 weeks until response.
- IDOT District 1: no published turnaround. Carry an allowance of at least 8–12 weeks in bid schedule planning for complete packages plus one resubmittal cycle, clearly identify it as a contractor planning assumption, **not IDOT SLA**. More if access-controlled work, variance, bridge attachment, detour, environmental, winter exception, active capital projects, or Area Coordinator handoff is involved.
- Rail license (UP/CSX/NS) and water/floodway permits: independent owners/agencies; no general permit clock determined here. Carry separate critical path; no notice to proceed until written license/permit and ROW agreement. Avoid making 8–12 weeks a promise for rail/USACE/water.

Potential schedule blockers are: (1) no title/ROW map for the arterial crossing; (2) fees/franchise applicability or $500/LF long-parallel route basis in EGV; (3) missing utility data / pothole results; (4) State D1 winter restriction; (5) Cook County submission only 2–3 months before construction; (6) rail crossing rights, signal protection and casing profile; (7) Salt Creek/floodway or Spur4 streambed determination; (8) local resurfacing moratoria / road jobs / utility cut bans; (9) route alignment currently unlabeled/unverified and terminal endpoint questions.

## Mandatory permit decision gates before fixed-price response

1. **Correct the route-to-road model.** Survey/station the 3 exact IDOT crossings, County Devon and Landmeier, and all potential road parallels. Overlay legal ROW boundaries, not only road centerlines. Produce one station map per authority with ROW edges, boundary transitions, crossing skew and bore pits.
2. **Ask the permittee/owner.** Confirm that Lightpath or named utility is the network owner/operator and can be permittee, holds ICC authorization if needed, has local franchises/licenses, carries insurance to municipal minimums and can provide security/bond.
3. **Get written fee determinations.** Elk Grove $1,500 application exception and $500/LF parallel duct-package fee; County application/permit fees and bond; D1 current application/fees; DuPage and Itasca fee/insurance/security. Identify which fee is per application vs linear footage vs permit coverage.
4. **Utility coordination package.** Request existing fiber, water, sewer, storm, streetlight, signal, gas, electric and rail as-builts; conduct SUE, JULIE design-quality tickets, site survey/potholes, and show confidence/source. Need utility data for each proposed route station; if agencies demand all utilities shown, this meets it while supporting corridor-wide route exhibits.
5. **Separate water and railroad.** Water/wetland/floodway scope by station and spoil/pit acreage; FRA crossing, rail engineering, title, flagging, insurance and license by crossing. Get owner/path clearance before setting route.
6. **Check program conflicts/moratoria.** Current pavement management, planned 2026–2030 improvements, resurfacing permit freeze, night/weekend work window, work-zone capacity, school/industrial traffic, and municipal event/holiday blackout at each road crossing.
7. **Price per authority and construction method.** Separate engineering by each named segment, separate permit production/coordination, utility SUE/potholes, rail crossing packages, water delineation/drill design, bonds/fees/reimbursables, and one allowance for comment cycles. Quote route optimization as a feasibility task with endpoints held fixed.
