## S1-S033 | Fred L. Stults Bridge over O’Shaughnessy Reservoir

**Advance a bridge attachment as the lead option.** Define the installation as three separate design packages on one profile: west landfall and approach, supported conduit on the bridge, east transition to the existing route and SR 257 vicinity. The original Water-labeled feature measures about 1,603 ft. ODOT SFN **2134462** is about 40 ft from the ODOT flowline hit and its inventory length is about 1,155 ft. The remaining distance can include alignment differences and off-structure transitions. Measure each component on the as-built plan. ODOT’s “Scioto River” record here is an artificial flow path through the reservoir. FEMA returned Zone A at the mapped point, and NWI returned lake polygon L1UBHh. [ODOT inventory and route evidence](water_crossing_point_evidence.csv)

**The agreement gives the review a starting point.** Delaware County Commissioners Resolution **01-957**, August 27, 2001, approves a County–City cooperative agreement for replacement and maintenance. The text identifies City-owned land under portions of the approach roadway and bridge, designates County Engineer and Commissioners as lead for the improvement, gives Columbus plan review and approval for that project, and sets out shared Phase One maintenance and replacement funding. Resolution 24-937 records the 2003 replacement and bridge naming. The tax GIS places the center hit in Delaware source parcel **500310-0, OID 74513**. Pull the recorded acquisition/easement, executed agreement and later amendments before deciding who signs or where surface and subsurface rights end. The 2001 project approvals do not by themselves authorize third-party telecom. ## S1-S029 at 659.5 ft | Olentangy River at Home Road

**Advance an attachment feasibility review and an HDD comparison in parallel.** SFN **2131870** is 64 ft from the mapped river hit. County project records identify Home Road bridge widening near SR 315. The atlas’s nearest road sample references SR 315 at the water point, while the structure inventory names Home Road. Draw the state highway interface on the approach plan and route the bridge request through Delaware County. FEMA returns **AE floodway**. The County’s bridge approach plan, not the stream intersection, sets the attachment and traffic transitions. [Delaware bridge widening](https://engineer.co.delaware.oh.us/projects/home315/)

**Attachment** can avoid in-channel construction if the approvals, access and deck or bridge mounting geometry all work. Request spare conduit and bridge utility records first. Compare existing duct reuse and a sealed supported conduit with independent transitions that clear utilities and the SR 315 interface. Have the County and Corps review navigation clearance, flood debris, operation, temporary access and actual §10 work. **HDD** avoids loading the bridge. It needs a geotechnical section below scour and abutment influences, upland pit/access rights and a drill-fluid contingency. **Another bridge crossing** retains the scenic river and may add major road frontage. The best use of engineering time is testing the two methods at Home Road first.

ODNR lists this reach in the Olentangy State Scenic River corridor, and Huntington lists the Olentangy under its Ohio Section 10 summary. Ask ODNR about §1517.15 applicability based on the public sponsor, municipal boundary, structure work and actual channel modifications. The ODNR/USFWS mussel protocol is a planning constraint for activities that require survey. The ordinary May 1–October 1 window can shift with agency authorization, and survey applicability depends on the selected method and actual impacts. Confirm handling of in-water work with ODNR Wildlife/USFWS before assuming a survey-season deadline. [ODNR scenic segment](https://ohiodnr.gov/go-and-do/plan-a-visit/find-a-property/olentangy-scenic-river) · [Ohio Revised Code §1517.15](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1517.15) · [ODNR/USFWS survey protocol](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/ohiodnr.gov/documents/wildlife/permits/dow-protocol-ohio-mussel-survey.pdf)

## County submittals and nearby work

Delaware County’s published utility process calls for plans and application **4–8 weeks before work** and states the standard utility permit has no fee. That window is for routine utility submittals. The plan should dimension ROW and pavement, show work and restoration, burial method, and use attached drawings to tie the crossing to identifiable locations. The standard form calls for 48 inches minimum depth and bored hard-surface road crossings absent separate pavement-cut consideration. Bridge, scour and utility clearances are separate design controls. [Delaware published permit process](https://engineer.co.delaware.oh.us/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2025/07/Public_Utility_Permit_Process.pdf) · [Utility application](https://engineer.co.delaware.oh.us/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2025/07/utility_permit_revised.pdf)

Union County’s April 2026 road work requirements treat location by back of ROW and include a ditch-bottom depth requirement. Treat 60-ft Watkins widths as section-specific records to obtain, with exceptions and private-use eligibility to resolve under the County rules. The Marysville Scottslawn–US 42 water-main project has a planned late 2027 or early 2028 start and a future water plant site on Watkins Road. Have Marysville provide the utility alignment and staging in this corridor before selecting pits or the Scottslawn alternative.
