## 6. Environmental, flood, drainage, and water review

The NWI screen shows mapped freshwater resources touching many features even when the customer calls the feature “Bore.” In Segment 1, water-adjacent or NWI-intersecting lines include S1-S028, S1-S029, S1-S030, S1-S032–S1-S034, S1-S036, S1-S038–S1-S042. This creates a separate check for bore pits, receiving pits, workspace, spoil, access, dewatering, tree clearing, and erosion controls; it is not enough to inspect only the six `Water` labels.

The long reservoir crossing is the design driver. Do not assume HDD avoids regulation: work pads/access and temporary fill can trigger permits, and inadvertent returns need a response plan. The design basis must include bore length/curve radius, cover below channel/bed, geotechnical uncertainty, entry/exit access, frac-out controls, water level variation, land access, and owner acceptance. Compare feasible alternatives only after these facts are assembled.

Ohio EPA’s official 401/isolated-wetland guidance states that discharges of dredged/fill material to US waters require Corps Section 404 and state 401 certification; isolated-wetland fill requires an Ohio EPA isolated-wetland permit. Its page also states 15 business days for an application completeness determination, not for a final permit decision, and provides published 401 fees ($200 application plus resource-based review fees). Actual applicability, current fees, applicable nationwide permit/certification, impact measurement, mitigation and total processing time require agency confirmation. [Ohio EPA 401 and Isolated Wetland Permits](https://epa.ohio.gov/divisions-and-offices/surface-water/permitting/water-quality-certification-and-isolated-wetland-permits). NWI is explicitly a mapping inventory, not a regulatory determination; confirm with current field delineation and agency process. [FWS NWI disclaimer](https://www.fws.gov/page/wetlands-geodatabase-disclaimer).

**Open flood review:** The earlier FEMA NFHL service attempt did not return reliable data. No “no floodplain” conclusion is made. Re-run current FEMA NFHL and Delaware/Union county/township floodplain layers in QGIS; overlay FEMA effective panels, floodway, dams/reservoir limits, ODNR water resources and ditch records; log source version/date and each crossing’s flood zone. The county/municipal floodplain authority must confirm permits for pits/access or grading in regulated zones.

6. **Field and specialty investigations.** Walk entire route in daylight, record gate/access, ditch/culvert, slopes, pavement/sidewalk, tree and staging constraints; survey vertical profiles; geotechnical investigation for long HDD/reservoir/interstate/rail; confirm floodplain and bank lines; investigate archaeological/historic and species constraints if right-of-way/permit footprints warrant.
7. **Method/alternatives engineering.** For each crossing, compare open-cut (only where permitted and safely constructible), HDD, jack-and-bore, bridge/structure attachment only after owner/structural approval, and endpoint-preserving deviations. ### 7.2 Critical path and schedule logic

Do in parallel during the first week: alignment/RFP clarifications, all ROW record searches, ODOT/CSX/county/township concept inquiries, water/road crossing profile and desktop utility/parcel review. The Delaware County public utility process requires applications **4–8 weeks before the planned work date**, so those permits are a construction schedule lead, not something to start at mobilization. CSX and complex US 33/36 interchange requirements have no substantiated duration yet and could become critical path. Ohio EPA notes an application completeness step of 15 business days for 401 applications, but that is not the full authorization duration; avoid substituting that figure for final review time. County permit process, seasonal restrictions, missing ROW, and application revisions may add more time.

**10-day bid-response plan (Nov 12–22, subject to Lightpath resource decisions):**

| Window | Segment 1 output | Gate / owner decision |
|---|---|---|
| Nov 12–13 | Freeze source quantities; send internal request for A/Z/spec, route gap, construction assumptions. Prepare route-stationed record request lists. | Customer route baseline and design basis. |
| Nov 13–16 | Retrieve online records and current project/moratorium layers; compile ROW plan index and road-owner map; stage ODOT/CSX/county inquiry packages. | Decide whether any route deviation is worth recommending. |
| Nov 16–18 | Complete field/aerial review; prepare crossing concept sheets for S1-S029, S1-S031, S1-S033, S1-S037/39/41 and S1-S043/S1-S045. Obtain any available written responses. | Gate on P0 reservoir; alternate path feasibility. |
| Nov 18–20 | Finish route/permit matrix, cost and schedule assumptions, alternatives comparison and risk register. Identify all pending agency response gaps with conservative assumptions and exclusions. | Commercial review and estimator sign-off. |
| Nov 21 | QA EDPA, maps, CSVs, route change KMZ (if justified), source manifest, pricing consistency and qualifications. | Independent GIS and engineering check. |
| Nov 22 | Submit response according to email instructions. | Confirm recipient/correspondence list and package integrity. |

This is a bid-development plan, not a construction schedule. Agency reply timing cannot be guaranteed in the ten-day bid window; mark unconfirmed standards/durations as pending and price coordination rather than inventing them.
